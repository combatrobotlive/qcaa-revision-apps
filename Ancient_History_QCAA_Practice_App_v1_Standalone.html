People, power and authority — personality source practice

The current Ancient History EA focuses on the school-selected Unit 4 personality: Julius Caesar or Cleopatra VII. Practise reading unfamiliar source evidence, using context statements, analysing motives and power, evaluating usefulness/reliability, corroborating sources and building a historical argument.

2026 format to practise

15 minutes planning · 120 minutes working · paragraph responses · evidence from provided historical sources.

The QCAA example book shows 3 questions and 6 sources for each selectable personality. The syllabus itself allows up to 12 sources and does not prescribe a fixed number of questions or objective weightings.

Most important habit: source judgments must answer the question. “Plutarch wrote later” is only a starting observation. Explain how the source’s origin, purpose, perspective or context strengthens or limits its evidence for the specific issue being asked.

Exam toolkit

Historical source skills

Analyse evidence

  • Identify the specific feature of the evidence that matters.
  • Explain what it reveals about the personality, power, motive, relationship or historical issue.
  • Use the context statement where it changes how the evidence should be read.
  • Keep connecting the explanation to the wording of the question.

Judge reliability

  • Make a decision about the degree of reliability.
  • Weigh a strength against a limitation where appropriate.
  • Use origin, proximity, access, purpose, genre, perspective and corroboration.
  • Judge reliability for the question asked, not the whole source in general.

Judge usefulness

  • Ask: useful for answering what?
  • Explain the specific information or perspective the source contributes.
  • Identify what it cannot establish and why that matters.
  • A biased source can still be highly useful for studying propaganda or perspective.

Synthesise

  • Start with a defensible historical judgment.
  • Combine sources around ideas, not one source per paragraph.
  • Account for conflicting or partial evidence.
  • Explain corroboration: what claim is supported by both sources and how?

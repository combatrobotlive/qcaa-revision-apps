WHAT MAKES DANCE DIFFERENT?

You cannot revise it like a content-heavy exam

The core skill is moving from observation → accurate terminology → interrelationship → meaning → justified judgment. The app therefore uses original animated storyboards instead of pretending that flashcards alone can prepare students for an unseen-video essay.

2025 REPORT FOCUS

The concept named in the question is the driver

High-level responses maintained precise focus on the prescribed dance concept or skill, described multiple characteristics, explained how they interacted, connected them to meaning and then made supported judgments.

2025-style mark map: Understanding dance concepts and skills 2 · Analysing and interpreting 16 · Evaluating and justifying 12 · Applying written literacy 3 = 33 marks. The app treats this as a practice blueprint, not a promise that a future paper will use the identical raw-mark layout.

REFERENCE

Dance EA guide

Current syllabus

Unit 4 is Moving my way. Students investigate how choreographers' movement styles reflect personal contexts, influences and perspectives, while developing understanding of personal movement style and viewpoints.

External assessment

  • 25% of the subject result.
  • Written analytical essay.
  • Unseen question selected from options and succinct unseen stimulus with contextual information.
  • Students must analyse, interpret, evaluate and justify.
  • 20 minutes planning time + 120 minutes working time.

What the 2025 paper looked like

  • One paper worth 33 raw marks.
  • Choice of two extended-response questions.
  • Both questions required analysis of unseen video excerpts from two different dance works.
  • Contextual information was supplied.

2025 subject-report advice

  • Make the concept or skill named in the question the explicit driver of the response.
  • Use precise characteristics and detailed movement examples.
  • Explain interrelationships between concepts/skills rather than treating them as isolated lists.
  • Plan key moments for analysis, interpretation and evaluation before writing.
  • Maintain a coherent line of reasoning; high-level responses interweave analysis, interpretation and evaluation.
  • Avoid lengthy general introductions. Get to the key points in the question.

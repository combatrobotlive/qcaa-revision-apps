Issues, ideas and attitudes

Track a text’s issue and viewpoint. Identify values, assumptions and attitudes, then explain how words, images, structure and medium invite audiences to take up a position.

Persuasive texts

Use a contention, logical sequence, relevant evidence, deliberate modality and rhetorical choices. A persuasive response is strongest when its voice and register fit its intended audience.

Comparing responses

Compare texts through a purposeful point of comparison: representation, perspective, audience positioning, context or technique — not simply a feature list.

Practice

IA1 extended-response support

IA1 is an examination — extended response on two different texts. Revision should target the three marking areas: Knowledge application (9), Organisation and development (9), and Textual features (7).

  1. Use the supplied texts and task wording to form a comparative contention.
  2. Make every paragraph analytical: evidence, technique, effect, perspective and positioning.
  3. Organise and sequence ideas with clear comparisons and cohesive links.
  4. Allow time to edit grammar, vocabulary, spelling, punctuation and paragraph control.

Quick reference

Contention: the central position of a persuasive text. Modality: language that shows degree of certainty or obligation (may, should, must). Juxtaposition: placing elements together to create contrast. Nominalisation: turning a verb/adjective into a noun, e.g. “decide” → “decision”. Paraphrase: express a source idea in your own words while acknowledging it.

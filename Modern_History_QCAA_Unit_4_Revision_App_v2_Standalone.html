HISTORICAL INQUIRY TOOLKIT

Source skills and planning

Analyse evidence

  • Identify a precise feature, not the entire source.
  • Explain explicit and/or implicit meaning in context.
  • Connect the inference to the historical question.
  • Do not simply paraphrase.

Evaluate reliability

  • Make a degree judgment for the inquiry.
  • Weigh origin, proximity, purpose and perspective.
  • Explain a relevant strength and limitation.
  • Do not use “biased” as a complete judgment.

Evaluate usefulness

  • Ask: useful for answering what?
  • Explain the specific contribution.
  • State what the evidence cannot establish.
  • Usefulness and reliability are not identical.

Synthesise evidence

  • Start with a qualified judgment.
  • Group evidence by ideas, not source order.
  • Explain corroboration, qualification or contradiction.
  • Return to the extent of the argument.

Historical source reminder

A source is material a historian examines. Evidence is information selected from a source to help answer a particular historical question. Primary and secondary sources can both be useful, and both need to be evaluated in relation to the inquiry.

UNIT 4 · TOPIC 2 · INDEPENDENT REHEARSAL

External exam simulator

Read the evidence. Plan your argument. Write under timed conditions.

Rehearse the revised 2026 example format: choose the topic studied by your class, use its six sources and answer three short-response questions in full-sentence paragraphs. The full timer gives 15 minutes of planning, then 120 minutes of working. The actual external assessment is handwritten; printing this paper lets you practise handwriting with the same timer.

Format, source provenance and official references

This is original educational practice, not a QCAA paper or a prediction of unseen questions. QCAA’s July 2026 examples show three questions and six sources per topic, in a combined question-and-stimulus book with a separate response book. The examples use placeholder marks, so this app does not invent official question weightings. Actual instructions and marking allocations take precedence.

The embedded stimulus cards are app-authored abridged evidence digests of verified, cited records, not verbatim quotations or facsimiles. Each has a distinct context statement. Historical document types and authors refer to the underlying records; the digests themselves are secondary representations. Practise reasoning from their evidence, but use authentic extracts and visual sources from your teacher for additional unseen-source practice. Short quotations, if shown, are separately identified. Linked originals are optional and are disabled during a timed attempt.

UNIT 4 REFERENCE

Close study of literary texts

Open official QCAA page

Scope: aligned to English General 2025 v1.3 (January 2026). This app is an independent revision resource, not a QCAA publication. Use the current syllabus, school task documents, QCAA prescribed text lists and subject notices as the final authority where applicable.

Topic 1 — Creative responses to literary texts

Use close study as a springboard for imaginative writing. Experiment with language, style, form, perspective, structure and aesthetic features to shape emotional and critical audience responses.

Topic 2 — Critical responses to literary texts

Develop original analytical interpretations through close study, whole-text evidence, cultural assumptions and values, aesthetic/stylistic effects and reasoned argument.

Exam flexibility

Practise adapting knowledge to unfamiliar questions rather than reproducing memorised essays; select evidence and argument in response to the exact task.

Key vocabulary

springboardA studied literary text used as the starting point for an imaginative response.
aesthetic featureA crafted element such as characterisation, setting, narrative voice, imagery, symbolism, motif, juxtaposition or structure that can shape response.
critical perspectiveAn informed, evidence-based interpretation that tests assumptions and accounts for complexity.
whole-text evidenceEvidence selected from patterns and developments across the text rather than an isolated moment.
unseen questionA question not known before the examination; preparation therefore needs flexible interpretation and evidence knowledge.
qualifierA word or phrase such as “ultimately”, “primarily”, “to what extent” or “despite” that changes the judgement required.

High-value revision routine

  1. Recall the concept or text evidence before opening notes.
  2. Answer the exact question in your own words.
  3. Reveal the checking guide and identify the missing reasoning.
  4. Revise the same response or retry a new A/B/C variant without copying a model.

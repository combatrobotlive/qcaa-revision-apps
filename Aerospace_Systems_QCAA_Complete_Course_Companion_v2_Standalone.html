IA2 contributes 25%. Practise regulatory systems, human performance, safety management, investigation and airline operations, including ASK, RPK, PLF, CASK, RASK, PRASK and yield.

This selects two question families per Unit 3 topic. It is a study set, not a complete QCAA assessment instrument. For timed full-paper practice, use the Unit 4 mock in its own section.

How to build a defensible solution

Explore: identify the problem, boundaries, interactions and evidence. Develop: compare and combine ideas against justified criteria. Generate: implement, simulate or hypothetically apply the proposal to produce data. Evaluate and refine: use that evidence throughout the process to justify changes.

Example: a claim that a new turnaround plan is faster needs a defined baseline, realistic task dependencies, repeated or justified simulated results, and evidence that safety and reliability remain acceptable.

REFERENCE

Course and external assessment guide

Source and scope

Course structure checked against QCAA Aerospace Systems 2025 v1.4 (March 2026), checked 10 September 2026. Original educational questions and synthetic data. Use official charts, the supplied QCAA formula material and your teacher's resources alongside this app.

Unit 1: Introduction to aerospace systems. Unit 2: Aerospace technologies. Unit 3: Aerospace ecosystems. Unit 4: Aircraft performance systems and human factors.

Current Unit 4 — five topics

  1. Airspace management — Airservices Australia, ATC, separation, UTC, emergency phases, VFR/IFR, VMC/IMC, flight plans, radio calls, aeronautical information and systems thinking.
  2. Aircraft performance — six key instruments, V-speeds, turbine engines, environmental impacts, altitude effects, pressure/density altitude and performance/loading charts.
  3. Aircraft maintenance — electrical, hydraulic, pressurisation, fuel and de-icing systems; line/base/shop maintenance; maintenance intervals and safety/economic relationships.
  4. Aircraft navigation & radio communication technologies — WAC/VNC/VTC, ERSA, SUA, GPWT, wind/track/time/fuel calculations, flight planning, EFIS/HUD, radar, approach guidance, ILS, ADS-B, TCAS and GNSS.
  5. Human performance & limitations — hypoxia/hyperventilation, decision-making, human error, fatigue/stress, cockpit ergonomics, CRM and situation awareness.

External assessment

The current syllabus specifies a written combination-response examination worth 25%. Questions may use multiple choice, single words, sentences/paragraphs, sketching/drawing/graphs/tables/diagrams, calculations and unseen stimulus.

The question allocation is approximately 60% simple familiar, 20% complex familiar and 20% complex unfamiliar (±2%).

Conditions: 5 minutes perusal and 120 minutes working. Students may use a non-programmable scientific and flight calculator and approved analog flight-planning tools; the QCAA Aerospace Systems formula/data book is provided.

Why the mock still uses 80 raw marks

The 2025 paper used 10 multiple-choice marks plus 70 short-response marks. That paper was under the previous topic structure, so this app uses the 80-mark shape only as a practice convention while replacing the content with the current five-topic syllabus. QCAA controls the actual 2026 instrument.

Useful habits from the latest subject report

  • Practise systems thinking habits and strategies rather than treating them as vocabulary only.
  • Do complex unfamiliar problems that require analysis, prioritised criteria, synthesis and justified recommendations.
  • Become fluent with aeronautical charts, weather products, ERSA-style information, flight plans, performance charts, calculators and plotters.
  • Break down the cognitive verb and every element of a short-response question before writing.

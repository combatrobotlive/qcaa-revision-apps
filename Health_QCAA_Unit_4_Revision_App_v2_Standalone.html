IA3 and external assessment support

Unit 4 contains IA3 Investigation (25%) and the QCAA external Examination — extended response (25%).

IA3 · Investigation

Individual written analytical exposition, up to 2000 words. Complete a local context analysis and needs assessment for the Year 12 cohort; evaluate two implemented innovations from outside the school setting using two RE-AIM steps and two diffusion variables; recommend one and develop a school diffusion action strategy.

IA3 · Decision logic

Evidence of cohort need → life course critique of determinants → fair comparison of two innovations → justified selection → methodology/resources that address a need, barrier or enabler and advocate, mediate or enable uptake.

External assessment

Written extended response: 15 minutes planning and 120 minutes working. It may include up to two unseen questions and unseen stimulus. Practise selecting only relevant evidence, interpreting trends and integrating the Health inquiry model into one sustained argument.

Quality checklist

Unit reference

Core language for accurate revision and assessment responses.

Affirmative consent
Free, voluntary, informed, mutual and communicated agreement that is ongoing and can be withdrawn.
Coercive control
A pattern of abusive behaviours used to dominate, isolate, intimidate or control another person.
Transition
A change in role or status that marks a distinct departure from a prior role or status.
Reverse transition
A return to a prior role or status, such as leaving home and later returning.
Trajectory
A long-term pattern of stability and change involving multiple transitions.
Life event
A significant occurrence involving relatively abrupt change and possible lasting effects.
Turning point
A life event or transition that produces a lasting shift in a trajectory.
Cohort
People born in a similar period who experience social changes in a related sequence and age.
Emerging adulthood
A transition period characterised by identity exploration, possibilities, feeling in between, self-focus and instability.
Age norms
Social and cultural expectations about the timing of life events and roles.

A quality-control statistics report

The length of a manufactured part is normally distributed with mean 250 mm and standard deviation 4.5 mm. Parts from 244 mm to 256 mm are accepted.

Find the proportion accepted and the length exceeded by the longest 5% of parts. In a separate random sample of 200 parts, 45 require rework. Construct an approximate 95% confidence interval for the rework proportion and interpret it.

Step 1: Decode and separate the report
  • The first two tasks use a continuous normal variable: write X∼N(250,4.5²).
  • The final task uses a sample proportion: p-hat=45/200=0.225.
  • Do not mix the normal-distribution standard deviation of lengths with the sampling standard error of a proportion.
Step 2: Model the accepted proportion
  • “From 244 mm to 256 mm” translates to P(244≤X≤256).
  • Use a normal CDF with lower bound 244, upper bound 256, mean 250 and standard deviation 4.5.
  • The result is about 0.818, so approximately 81.8% of parts are accepted under this model.
Check point: the interval is centred on the mean and extends about 1.33 standard deviations each way, so a result around 0.8 is plausible.
Step 3: Translate the upper-tail wording
  • “Longest 5%” is an upper tail, so the required cutoff has 95% of values below it.
  • Use inverse normal with cumulative probability 0.95, mean 250 and standard deviation 4.5.
  • The cutoff is approximately 257.4 mm. Values above this are in the longest 5%.
Step 4: Build and interpret the interval
  • Use p-hat ± 1.96√(p-hat(1−p-hat)/n).
  • Here this is approximately 0.225 ± 0.058, giving about (0.167, 0.283).
  • Interpret in context: based on this sample, a plausible population rework proportion is about 16.7% to 28.3%. Do not claim that exactly this many individual parts will require rework.
EXAM-STYLE

Now try a similar question with less help

A mass measurement follows Y∼N(510,8²) grams. Find the proportion from 500 g to 522 g and the mass exceeded by the heaviest 2.5%. A random sample of 160 items contains 24 defects; construct and interpret an approximate 95% confidence interval for the defect proportion.

Compare against a sensible plan
  1. Write the normal model and distinguish an interval probability from an inverse-normal cutoff.
  2. Translate “heaviest 2.5%” to a left-tail probability of 0.975.
  3. Calculate p-hat, standard error and the interval; finish with a population-level interpretation.

Use a blank page to write: given → model → target → method → check. The aim is to make your thinking visible before you calculate.

CALCULATOR SKILLS

Unit 4 calculator skills

Use this as a workflow guide, not a substitute for working. The key names differ between calculator models, but the process is the same: enter the expression carefully, keep full precision while working, then check that the answer suits the graph, domain, units and context. Use the calculator approved for your assessment and follow your school and QCAA conditions.
Before you calculate
  • Use brackets for every bound, denominator and probability expression. Keep full calculator precision until the final sentence.
  • Write the distribution, parameters and units before using any statistics command; this prevents entering a standard deviation where a variance is required.
  • For triangle questions, use degree mode when the given angles are in degrees; do not reuse a radian-mode setting from calculus work.
Definite integrals and area
  • Use numerical integration to check F(b)−F(a). It gives signed area, so inspect the graph and split at each crossing when total area is required.
  • For area between curves, graph both rules, find their intersection(s), then enter upper−lower over each correct interval.
  • For the trapezoidal rule, use a list/table carefully: endpoints occur once and internal ordinates occur twice.
Normal distributions
  • Use a normal CDF command for an interval probability: lower bound, upper bound, mean μ, then standard deviation σ.
  • Use inverse normal only when given a cumulative probability. Translate wording such as “top 10%” into the correct left-tail probability first.
  • Check that a probability is between 0 and 1 and that a calculated value sits sensibly relative to the mean and standard deviation.
Sampling and proportions
  • For a standard deviation of p-hat, enter sqrt(p(1−p)/n); use the population proportion p or the stated estimate exactly as the question requires.
  • For an interval, calculate centre ± margin and keep both endpoints in context: a proportion should remain between 0 and 1.
  • Use calculator output to support a written conclusion about the sample, population and uncertainty; it does not supply the interpretation for you.

Four-step calculator routine

  1. Sketch/graph first when an integral asks for area.
  2. Enter normal-distribution bounds in the correct order with μ and σ.
  3. Translate upper-tail wording before using inverse normal.
  4. State the final statistical conclusion in context and round sensibly.

Calculator output is an approximation unless a question explicitly asks for an exact result. Show the mathematical method and use the calculator to obtain, check or interpret numerical values.

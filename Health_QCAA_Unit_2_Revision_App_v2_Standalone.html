Define and understand

Use local primary data and credible secondary data to establish the significance of a narrow alcohol/other drugs or body-image issue in a peer/family context.

Plan and implement

Apply social cognitive theory and the Ottawa Charter to select a protective approach, justify the methodology and resources, and plan RE-AIM data collection.

Evaluate and reflect

Compare pre/post evidence, judge impact on peer/family resources, then justify a recommendation that advocates, mediates or enables further action.

Quality checklist

Unit reference

Core language for accurate revision and assessment responses.

Social cognitive theory
The dynamic interaction of individual, environmental and behavioural influences.
Observational learning
Learning a behaviour by observing a role model.
Facilitation
Providing tools, resources or environmental changes that make a behaviour easier.
Incentive motivation
Using anticipated rewards or consequences to influence behaviour.
Collective efficacy
A group’s shared belief that it can act successfully.
Risk factor
A factor associated with increased likelihood of a harmful health outcome or behaviour.
Protective factor
A factor that reduces risk or strengthens health resources.
Moral disengagement
Ways people disconnect behaviour from personal moral standards to justify harmful action.

Cognitive verb moves

VerbWhat the response must do
AnalyseBreak information into parts, explain relationships and draw a conclusion.
InterpretMake meaning from data trends or statements and state what the evidence shows.
CritiqueExamine information in detail through the relevant model to distinguish determinants.
SynthesiseCombine evidence into a new, coherent action strategy or decision.
EvaluateJudge methodology or resources against criteria such as RE-AIM.
ReflectAppraise impact or uptake and use the judgment to shape future action.
JustifySupport a decision or recommendation with relevant evidence and reasoning.

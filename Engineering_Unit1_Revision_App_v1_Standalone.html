Mechanics fundamentals

Weight and forceW = mg
F = maUse g = 9.8 m/s² unless another value is supplied.
Force componentsFH = F cos θ
FV = F sin θCheck how the angle is measured before choosing sine or cosine.
Perpendicular resultantFT = √(FH² + FV²)Direction can be found from tan⁻¹(FV/FH), with quadrant checked.
MomentsM = Fd⊥
ΣM = 0Use the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force line of action.
EquilibriumΣFx = 0
ΣFy = 0
ΣM = 0The equilibrant is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the resultant.
Beam reactionsΣM = 0, then ΣFy = 0Unit 1 questions use vertical point loads and simple pin/roller support models.

Communication, materials and data

Densityρ = m / VConvert mass and volume to compatible units. 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.
Drawing scaleactual = drawing × scale denominatorAt 1:20, 1 mm on the drawing represents 20 mm actual.
Strength-to-weightratio = weight held / project weightState how failure load and project weight were measured.
Engineering viewsorthographic · isometric · obliqueUse scale, units, title, orientation, dimensions, parts lists and callouts to remove ambiguity.
Material classesmetals/alloys · polymers · ceramics · composites · natural materialsSelection requires properties, manufacture, environment and life-cycle context.
Primary bondingionic · covalent · metallicBonding helps explain broad properties such as conductivity, brittleness and ductility.

A repeatable force-and-moment method

  1. Identify the body or component to analyse.
  2. Draw a clear free-body diagram and replace supports with the correct reaction components.
  3. Mark force magnitude, direction, point of application and relevant distances.
  4. Resolve angled forces before calculating resultants or reactions.
  5. Choose a sign convention and apply force or moment equilibrium.
  6. Check the units, direction and physical reasonableness of the answer.

Written-response method

  1. Answer the command word: identify, describe, explain, analyse, compare or justify.
  2. Use the named engineering concept accurately.
  3. Link the concept to the supplied component, user or community context.
  4. When recommending, identify both a benefit and a relevant trade-off or limitation.

Checking answers

Use one number per field. Examples: 12.5 kN, 12500 N, 36 N m, 2700 kg/m³. A bare number uses the unit shown beside the field. Most numerical checks allow 1.5% rounding tolerance. Automatic marks check final answers; students should still show working for assessment preparation.

Practice exam conditions

The generated 50-mark mock is a revision blueprint with 30 simple-familiar marks, 10 complex-familiar marks and one 10-mark coherent CU scenario. The raw total is a local classroom choice rather than a prescribed QCAA Unit 1 examination.

Boundaries

The app intentionally stops at Unit 1: point-force resultants, simple vertical beam reactions, moments, truss/load-path recognition, density, drawing scale, strength-to-weight ratio and qualitative stress–strain interpretation. It does not introduce Unit 2 circuit/logic calculations, Unit 3 method of joints/sections, SFD/BMD, stress/strain calculations or Young's modulus calculations.

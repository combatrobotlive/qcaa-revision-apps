A reliable calculation chain

Read representation → record quantities and units → choose equation → substitute → calculate → check physical meaning. Show your working even when the final answer is auto-checked.

Useful conversions

1 kN = 1000 N; 1 kN m = 1000 N m; 1 N m = 1000 N mm; 1 mm = 0.001 m; 1 mm3 = 0.000000001 m3. Cubic units need the cubed conversion factor.

What is the examiner asking?

Subject-specific tools

Read a technical drawing

Read title, units, stated scale, view direction and dimensions. Dimension labels show actual sizes; only a separately stated paper measurement needs scale conversion. Never estimate a missing dimension from a screen image.

Use a complete free-body diagram

Identify the body, support type, applied forces and distances. For Unit 1 beam reaction problems, external loading is vertical. Check force balance and moment balance using one declared sign convention.

Components and resultants

Angles are measured from the marked reference line. For a horizontal reference use Fx = F cos theta and Fy = F sin theta, then assign direction/sign. An equilibrant is equal and opposite to the resultant.

Mass, weight and density

Mass is in kg; weight is a force in N. Use g = 9.8 m/s2 when specified. Density uses mass / volume. Convert lengths before multiplying, or cube the conversion factor for a volume.

Testing and spreadsheets

Record units and all valid trials. Compare supported load / project weight using two forces. Retain the original table, choose honest graph scales and describe variability; do not hide an inconvenient failed test.

Read stress-strain features qualitatively

Recognise proportional/elastic and plastic behaviour, yield, UTS and fracture. Compare stiffness using initial slope on common axes and ductility using strain to fracture. Unit 1 does not require numerical stress, Young’s modulus or factor-of-safety calculations.

Evidence-based decisions and ethics

State a recommendation, quote relevant evidence, compare it with the brief and explain a limitation. Report failures honestly, practise within competence and consider community, environmental and safety consequences.

Practical and report preparation

Develop criteria, sketch alternatives, communicate with drawings, build/test a prototype and evaluate it. The quiz rehearses thinking but cannot replace the practical prototype and material-testing evidence expected in the unit.

Unit boundary

Four topics: Engineering in society; Engineering communication; Introduction to engineering mechanics; Introduction to engineering materials. Full truss-force analysis, SFD/BMD, stress/modulus calculations, circuits and phase diagrams belong elsewhere. C06 is retained only as a labelled legacy angled-support-reaction extension.

Study of Religion EA guide

2026 Unit 4 Topic 2

Human existence and rights asks students to explain features and expressions of religious traditions, analyse perspectives, and evaluate the significance and influence of religion when responding to ultimate questions, social justice and human rights.

The five major world religions in the topic are Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism and Buddhism.

External assessment conditions

  • Examination — short response, worth 25% of the subject.
  • 15 minutes planning time + 120 minutes working time.
  • No notes.
  • Unseen written and/or visual stimulus may include sacred/religious writing, reference texts, media, images, art, diagrams, graphs or tables.
  • Responses may range from sentences/paragraphs to several developed paragraphs.

2026 change to remember

The topic was renamed from Religion and human rights to Human existence and rights. QCAA states that the assessable subject matter has broadened to examine perspectives on ultimate questions, social justice and human rights.

What the 2025 subject report says to strengthen

  • Use planning time to allocate time across the whole paper and annotate stimulus, including context statements.
  • Use stimulus to support the explanation or analysis; do not simply summarise it.
  • Avoid listing everything remembered about a tradition. Select teachings that directly answer the question.
  • For higher-mark responses, explain relevant teachings in detail and explicitly link them to the stimulus and question.
  • Organise responses purposefully with topic sentences and concise concluding judgments where appropriate.

Practice-source integrity

Sacred-text material in this app is paraphrased for revision unless explicitly labelled otherwise. Fictional data and scenarios are identified as practice material. Students should use their class notes and authoritative sources for exact sacred-text quotations and tradition-specific detail.

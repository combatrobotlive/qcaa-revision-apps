Best way to use this app

First set your class topics in the left panel. Then practise a source skill, reveal the guidance after planning your answer, and rate whether you could do it independently.

The aim is not to memorise generic source comments: every judgment needs to explain why that source is useful or reliable for this exact inquiry.

Unit boundary: this V2 app follows the QCAA Unit structure but does not replace your school’s selected content. It deliberately adapts to the site, society, personality or historical period chosen by your class.

Historical inquiry toolkit

Source skills and planning

Analyse evidence

  • Identify a precise feature of the source.
  • Explain its explicit or implicit meaning.
  • Connect the inference to the historical question.
  • Do not simply retell what the source says.

Evaluate reliability

  • Give a degree judgment for the inquiry.
  • Weigh origin, proximity, purpose and perspective.
  • Identify a strength and a relevant limitation.
  • Avoid calling a source simply “biased”.

Evaluate usefulness

  • Ask: useful for answering what?
  • Explain the contribution it makes.
  • Identify what it cannot establish.
  • Usefulness and reliability are not identical.

Synthesise evidence

  • Start with a defensible judgment.
  • Group sources around ideas, not source order.
  • Explain corroboration or tension precisely.
  • Account for qualified or conflicting evidence.

Topic 1: Creative responses to literary texts

Use literary structures, language and style to reimagine perspectives. Explore how transformation across genre or medium changes meaning and repositions audiences.

Topic 2: Critical responses to literary texts

Develop and test your interpretation through close study of textual details and other interpretations. Sustain an analytical point of view about how choices shape meaning.

Close study habits

Annotate patterns: imagery, motif, symbol, setting, characterisation, narrative voice, dialogue, contrast, omissions and structure. Link each choice to representation and audience effect.

Practice

Close-study response toolkit

For imaginative writing: select a purposeful springboard, voice, genre, structure and ending; make transformation choices visible in the finished text. For critical writing: formulate an interpretation, select short precise evidence, analyse patterns and effects, and address alternative interpretations where useful.

Useful planning question: What does this text make audiences notice, value, question or feel — and how?

Quick reference

Motif: a recurring element that develops meaning. Symbol: an object/image/action that carries associated ideas. Narrative voice: the perspective and style through which a story is told. Textual integrity: a text’s enduring coherence and value in its context. Transformation: reshaping ideas, perspectives or conventions into another text, genre or medium.

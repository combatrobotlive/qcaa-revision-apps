What the current syllabus expects

Analyse

Identify significant characteristics and interrelationships between key concepts, then interpret how those relationships communicate meaning.

Evaluate

Reach a clear judgment about production practices and how effectively they communicate conceptual, technical or creative purpose.

Use context

Make inferences about contexts of production and use; don't just repeat the contextual blurb.

Use evidence

Name precise moments, film elements and relationships that directly support your viewpoint.

2026 change

QCAA states the revised syllabus reflects a move towards plain language and has no material effect on the external assessment.

The examination conditions no longer specify a word length. This app therefore does not enforce the 2025 paper's 800–1000-word instruction.

2025 marker advice built into this app

Don't evaluate as “strengths and limitations”.
Form a viewpoint about the actual question and use evidence to justify conclusions.
Context must become inference.
Explain how production/use circumstances shape the choices, audience or meaning.
Evidence must be film evidence.
Specific camera, editing, sound, mise-en-scène, performance, graphic or platform features — not vague plot summary.
Analyse relationships.
Shot + sound + editing + representation working together is stronger than a technique list.

FTVNM Reference

Best analytical sentence pattern: precise evidence → relationship between film elements/key concepts → interpretation of meaning → contextual inference → judgment linked to the question.

Five key concepts

Technologies

Tools, processes and technical possibilities/constraints used to make, distribute and experience moving-image media.

Representations

How people, places, events, ideas and emotions are constructed rather than simply reproduced.

Audiences

How products target, position, engage and are interpreted or used by individuals and groups.

Institutions

Organisations, platforms, regulation, funding, distribution, ownership and industrial practices.

Languages

Signs, symbols, codes and conventions that organise audio-visual meaning.

Useful terminology

Analyse

Identify significant characteristics and relationships, then interpret how the elements work together to communicate meaning.

Avoid: “There is a close-up. There is dark lighting. There is music.”
Better: explain how those features interact and what that interaction makes the audience understand or feel.

Evaluate

Use analysis to reach a supported judgment about the effectiveness or significance of production practices in relation to the question.

Avoid framing the whole response as strengths versus limitations unless the question actually asks for that.

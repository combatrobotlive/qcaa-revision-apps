Symbols, units and chemistry notation

Read the context as well as the symbol. The table below includes official notation plus teaching reminders needed to use the book correctly.

Symbol / notationMeaning in this guideCheck before using it
nAmount of substance.Unit mol. Do not confuse with a coefficient in a balanced equation.
mMass.Usually g when M is in g mol−1; keep units consistent.
MMolar mass.g mol−1; calculate from the periodic table using the chemical formula.
cMolarity / concentration.mol dm−3 in the printed relationship.
VVolume of solution.Use dm3 with mol dm−3: 1000 cm3 = 1 dm3 = 1 L.
RFRetention factor for chromatography.Dimensionless. Measure spot and solvent-front distances from the same origin in the same units.
pIIsoelectric point of an amino acid.Compare buffer pH with pI to reason about net charge; pI itself is not a concentration.
cm−1Wavenumber in the IR data table.It is inverse centimetres, not wavelength in centimetres.
m/zMass-to-charge ratio in mass spectrometry.For a singly charged molecular ion, molecular-ion m/z can indicate relative molecular mass.
%Percentage yield, uncertainty or error depending on the heading.Read the numerator/denominator in the printed relationship; these percentages answer different questions.

Volume and amount conversions

1 dm³ = 1 L = 1000 cm³ = 1000 mL.

250 mL = 0.250 dm³.

For n = m/M, using mass in g with M in g mol⁻¹ returns n in mol.

The volume conversion is printed on reference page 1; the worked conversion examples are teaching additions.

Structural formula reminders

Check carbon valency and identify the functional group before naming or choosing a pathway.

Reaction arrows in the printed pathway diagram show class-to-class possibilities; they do not supply the reagent and condition for you.

IR evidence should support a structural proposal together with other evidence; one peak rarely proves a whole molecule.

Calculation chain for synthesis

Mass → moles: n = m/M.

Balanced equation: use the stoichiometric mole ratio to identify the limiting reagent and theoretical product.

Yield: experimental/theoretical × 100%.

The limiting-reagent workflow is a teaching method. It is required chemistry reasoning, not a separate printed “limiting reagent formula”.

Electrophoresis direction from pH and pI

pH < pI: the amino acid has a net positive charge and moves toward the negative electrode.

pH ≈ pI: net charge is approximately zero and migration is minimal.

pH > pI: net negative charge; movement is toward the positive electrode.

This reasoning is teaching guidance using the pI data supplied on printed pages 12–15.

Sources, scope and teaching additions

Official notation, page locations and reference values were checked against the QCAA Chemistry 2025 v1.3 Formula and data book and the Chemistry 2025 v1.3 syllabus on 26 October 2026.

The guide focuses on Unit 4 science understanding. Equilibrium, acid–base and electrochemistry relationships printed on page 3 belong to Unit 3 and are deliberately not taught in this Unit 4 guide. The reaction-pathway diagram is included in the current 2026 formula/data booklet; reagents and reaction conditions remain subject knowledge to apply.

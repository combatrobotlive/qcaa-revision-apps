Unit focus

Respond to and analyse perspectives represented in Australian texts, including how cultural assumptions, values, attitudes and beliefs underpin them. Create imaginative and analytical texts for different contexts and audiences.

Australian perspectives

Identify the perspective represented, then connect it to the text’s context, values and language choices. Australian texts may include texts by Aboriginal writers or Torres Strait Islander writers.

Representation and point of view

Analyse how characters, settings, themes and narrative point of view construct identities and groups. Consider which voices are centred or marginalised.

Analytical and imaginative responses

Use a purposeful structure, precise metalanguage and evidence. In imaginative work, choose language and form that create a clear viewpoint for your intended audience.

Response toolkit

Build a critical paragraph: contention → evidence → technique → effect → perspective/value → audience response. For imaginative writing, establish a deliberate narrator/voice, audience relationship and purposeful ending.

Quick reference

Perspective: the way a text positions a reader/viewer, or an ideology embedded in a text. Point of view: the position from which a narrative is told. Assumption: an idea treated as true without being stated or proved. Metalanguage: subject vocabulary used to discuss texts and their composition.

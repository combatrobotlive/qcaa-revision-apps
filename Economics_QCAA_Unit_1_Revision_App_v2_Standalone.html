UNIT 1 · SCHOOL-BASED ASSESSMENT

Practical revision focus

  • Unit 1 assessment arrangements are set by schools. Revise the four unit objectives: comprehend, analyse, evaluate and create.
  • For stimulus questions, identify the economic issue, quantify a relevant pattern, explain a causal relationship using an appropriate model, then make a reasoned decision.
  • Practise labelled PPC, circular-flow and demand/supply representations; explain the diagram in surrounding economic language.
RESPONSE QUALITY

Evidence-to-decision sequence

  1. Identify the economic issue and quantify the most relevant evidence.
  2. Interpret the pattern or trend; do not merely repeat it.
  3. Explain the causal relationship using an appropriate model or economic concept.
  4. Use an economic criterion and acknowledge a relevant trade-off, limitation or perspective.
  5. Make an explicit and qualified decision.
REFERENCE

Unit 1 quick reference

Key calculations and relationships

Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100
PED = percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in price
Simple multiplier = 1 ÷ MPS = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)

Model discipline

  • PPC: distinguish a change in productive capacity from a movement caused by better use of existing resources.
  • Circular flow: saving, taxation and imports are leakages; investment, government spending and exports are injections.
  • Demand/supply: a change in the good’s own price is a movement along a curve; a non-price determinant shifts a curve.

Data and evidence

  • Use sources that are current, accurate and relevant to the particular issue.
  • Describe a pattern precisely, then explain why it matters economically.
  • Use percentage points when comparing two percentage rates; one percentage point equals 100 basis points.
  • State a limitation if evidence is partial, lagged, revised or does not establish causation.

Evaluation language

  • Choose an economic criterion appropriate to the decision.
  • Distinguish short- and long-term effects.
  • Consider households, businesses, government and other affected groups where relevant.
  • Reach a conditional conclusion supported by analysis, rather than a personal assertion.

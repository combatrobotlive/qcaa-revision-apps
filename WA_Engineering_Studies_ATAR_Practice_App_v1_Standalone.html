The Year 12 course is split 50:50 in the examination: everyone sits the Core section, then answers either the Mechanical or Mechatronics specialist field. This app keeps those pathways separate while sharing the common engineering calculations, design, materials, mechanisms, energy and life-cycle content.

2026 WA Engineering Studies ATAR guide

Course structure

The Year 12 syllabus is effective from 1 January 2026. Units 3 and 4 are examinable. Students study all Core content plus one specialist engineering field: Mechanical or Mechatronics.

Examination

  • Reading time: 10 minutes.
  • Working time: 3 hours.
  • Section One — Core: 50% total. Part A = 10%; Part B = 40%.
  • Section Two — chosen specialist field: 50% total. Part A = 10%; Part B = 40%.
  • Part A has 5–8 short-answer questions without parts.
  • Part B has 4–6 questions with parts that typically increase in complexity.
  • Data Book supplied by the supervisor.
  • Up to three permitted calculators.

Why it feels different from QCAA Engineering

The biggest structural difference is the specialist split. A Mechanical student can be doing method of sections, shear-force/bending-moment reasoning, second moment of area, beam deflection and kinematics, while a Mechatronics student can be doing transistor modes, voltage dividers, ADC conversion, PWM, motor drivers and flowcharts. Both still share a sizeable Core engineering paper.

2025 examiner feedback worth building habits around

  • Unit conversions were a common source of lost marks.
  • Formulae/equations and all workings should be shown clearly.
  • Justifications need ordered logic and must fit the actual scenario.
  • Drawings should use standard conventions, dimensions, centre lines and hidden detail.
  • Part B commonly links multiple ideas and requires several steps.
  • Mechatronics students particularly struggled with voltage dividers and transistor operation.

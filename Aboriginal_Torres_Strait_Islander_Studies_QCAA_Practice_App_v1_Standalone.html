UNIT 4 TOPIC 2

What the current syllabus asks students to know

Terminology including Constitution, racism, reconciliation, sovereignty, treaty and truth-telling; historical, social and cultural factors shaping reconciliation; major responses such as Bringing Them Home, the 2008 National Apology, Reconciliation Australia, the Royal Commission into Aboriginal Deaths in Custody, Closing the Gap, constitutional reform proposals, the Uluru Statement from the Heart and Reconciliation Action Plans.

THE CORE IDEA

Connectedness is not a side topic

Students need to use their understanding of continuing connections between land, language, culture, time, place and relationships in Aboriginal societies and Torres Strait Islander societies when interpreting recognition and reconciliation.

SOURCE SKILLS

Unseen stimulus drives the exam

Practice moves from identifying key ideas to interpreting evidence, comparing viewpoints and perspectives, explaining influence, and making reasoned judgments about significance and impact.

2025 REPORT ADVICE

Use evidence, not references

A strong response does more than mention “Source 2”. It selects evidence that directly supports the claim and explains how that evidence strengthens the reasoning.

REFERENCE

2026 EA guide and cultural-care notes

EA scope

External assessment is 25% and focuses on Unit 4 Topic 2: Recognition and reconciliation. Students respond in sentences or paragraphs to unseen text and visual stimulus.

Conditions: 15 minutes planning + 120 minutes working; written mode; no notes.

2026 objective emphasis

Define/use terminology; demonstrate understanding of continuing connectedness; interpret information from sources; analyse viewpoints and perspectives; evaluate significance and impact; create clear short responses.

Respectful language

  • Use Aboriginal peoples and Torres Strait Islander peoples when referring to diverse peoples collectively.
  • Capitalise terms such as Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander and Country/Place when used in this context.
  • Avoid treating Aboriginal cultures or Torres Strait Islander cultures as one uniform culture.
  • Use the terms preferred by the relevant community in local contexts.

Knowledge and permissions

Cultural knowledge is owned by particular people and communities. Publicly available “outside knowledge” is appropriate for a general revision app; local, sacred, restricted or community-specific knowledge requires the relevant authority and permission.

Useful response frame: make the claim → select exact evidence → explain what the evidence shows → connect it to the question → where asked, make a clear judgment about significance, extent or impact.

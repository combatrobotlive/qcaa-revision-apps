REFERENCE

Unit 4 quick reference

NPSC

final nutrient profiling score = baseline points − modifying points
  • Category 1 (beverages): final score must be < 1.
  • Category 2 (most foods): final score must be < 4.
  • Category 3 (specified cheeses/oils/spreads/butter): final score must be < 28.
  • Health claims are only permitted when the food meets the NPSC.

Useful label thresholds for practice

  • Low sodium (solid food): ≤ 120 mg/100 g.
  • Low sugar (solid food): ≤ 5 g/100 g.
  • Low saturated + trans fat (solid food): ≤ 1.5 g/100 g.
  • Good source of fibre: ≥ 4 g dietary fibre per serve.

NCM snapshot

  • Infant — nutrient-dense foods; age-appropriate texture.
  • Toddler — iron-rich; low salt/sugar.
  • Adolescent — calcium, iron, growth and energy needs.
  • Pregnant — folate, iron, iodine and food-safety constraints.
  • Elderly — nutrient density with lower energy needs; calcium/protein/fibre often relevant.
  • Vegetarian/vegan — protein, iron, calcium, zinc, B12, D.
  • Type 2 diabetes — lower GI and whole foods.
  • CHD/obesity — limit saturated fat; consider energy, salt and fibre/produce.

Extended-response sequence

  1. Define the NCM problem from stakeholder/data evidence.
  2. Analyse constraints and nutritional needs.
  3. Determine measurable success criteria.
  4. Synthesise multiple data sources to compare solutions.
  5. Evaluate against the success criteria.
  6. Refine and justify recommendations with data.
Exam habit: don't write “healthier” without saying how and for whom. Name the NCM requirement, quote the relevant numerical evidence, and connect the proposed component change to a nutritional or sensory consequence.

REFERENCE

Economics EA guide

2026 external assessment scope

  • Unit 4 Topic 1 — Macroeconomic objectives and theory.
  • Unit 4 Topic 3 — Economic management.
  • Written examination; 15 minutes planning + 120 minutes working.
  • QCAA-approved non-programmable calculator permitted.
  • Questions may use multiple choice, sentences, paragraphs, extended response, calculations, annotation, diagrams and unseen stimulus.

High-value formulas

% change = (new − old) / old × 100
inflation = % change in CPI
unemployment rate = unemployed / labour force × 100
participation = labour force / working-age population × 100
APC = C / Yd · APS = S / Yd
k = 1/MPS = 1/(1−MPC)
100 basis points = 1 percentage point

Core models

  • AD = C + I + G + (X − M)
  • AD–AS: distinguish demand shifts, SRAS cost shocks and LRAS productive-capacity changes.
  • PPC: distinguish an outward/inward shift from moving toward or along an existing frontier.
  • Circular flow: injections (I, G, X) and leakages (S, T, M).
  • Automatic stabilisers vs discretionary fiscal policy.

Evaluation sequence

  1. State the economic issue and quantify the key evidence.
  2. Analyse the causal relationship using an economic model.
  3. Explain each policy option’s transmission mechanism.
  4. Apply the stated economic criterion.
  5. Include limitations, time lags and trade-offs.
  6. Make an explicit decision.

Current-context habit

Economics Unit 4 expects familiarity with recent Australian conditions. Before the EA, update your benchmark knowledge from objective sources such as the ABS and RBA: inflation, unemployment, GDP growth, participation and cash-rate ranges.

The RBA inflation target remains 2–3% over time. Treat any exact current cash-rate value in classroom notes as date-sensitive.

2025 subject-report emphasis: responses should deconstruct the exact question, quantify data and dates, interpret rather than copy stimulus, make an explicit decision when evaluating, and connect familiar theory to unfamiliar economic situations.

2026 FOCUS

Two philosophies — assigned

The current EA requires analysis and evaluation of two of four philosophies: anarchism, libertarianism, social liberalism and socialism. The exam will tell students which two to address. The app therefore randomises the assigned pair rather than letting students avoid a weaker philosophy.

THE CORE MOVE

Assumptions → ideal society

Do not memorise isolated labels. Link each philosophy's assumptions about people, freedom, equality, rights, property and authority to its conception of legitimate institutions and the ideal society.

REASONING

Deconstruct, then evaluate

Students need to find premises, conclusions and hidden assumptions; recognise valid and invalid forms; identify fallacies; and evaluate truth, validity, soundness or inductive strength where appropriate.

STIMULUS

Respond to the problem

The 2025 subject report warned against rehearsed essays. Strong responses selected only the tenets relevant to the specific issue and directly engaged with arguments contained in the stimulus.

Important nuance: the four labels are philosophical families, not single frozen doctrines. The app avoids pretending, for example, that Bakunin and Rothbard share the same theory of property. Use the syllabus category while keeping internal differences visible.

Philosophy & Reason EA guide

2026 external assessment

  • Unit 4 Topic 2 — Political philosophy.
  • 25% external assessment.
  • Written analytical essay responding to an unseen problem, question or hypothesis and unseen stimulus.
  • 15 minutes planning + 120 minutes working time.
  • No notes.
  • Students analyse and evaluate two political philosophies and focus on the relationship between philosophical assumptions and each theory's conception of the ideal society.
  • The 2026 paper will specify the two philosophies. Students will not choose the pair.
  • The four current philosophies are anarchism, libertarianism, social liberalism and socialism.

2025 paper: useful practice pattern, not a 2026 promise

The 2025 paper was one 50-mark extended response. Libertarianism was preselected and students chose a second philosophy. That choice arrangement changes in 2026. The 2025 paper also specified 800–1000 words; current 2026 conditions do not specify a word length.

What QCAA said to strengthen

  • Read the cues in the question and directly engage with the ideas and arguments in the stimulus.
  • During planning, select the tenets actually relevant to the inquiry issue rather than dumping everything remembered about a philosophy.
  • Explain relationships: show how assumptions about human nature connect to freedom, equality, fairness and justice, and how those ideas shape government and power.
  • Do not treat premises as true merely because a philosopher would endorse them; evaluate their truth or plausibility on their own merits.
  • Be ready to apply reasoning skills from the syllabus to stimulus arguments.

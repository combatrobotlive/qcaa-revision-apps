Unit reference

Concise prompts for revision—not a replacement for the syllabus or class materials.

Consumer food drivers

  • Convenience
  • Budget
  • Perceived quality
  • Ethics and culture

Analyse how evidence about these drivers changes purchasing and product development.

Alternative food products

Relevant examples include genetically modified, organic, fortified, functional, insect-based, synthetic or 3D-printed foods and bush food sources. Judge safety, ethics, transparency, sustainability and consumer fit using evidence.

Sensory profiling

  • Triangle: identify whether an overall difference exists.
  • Duo-trio: determine which coded sample matches a reference.
  • Product profile: rate attribute intensity using a shared lexicon.

Deterioration and hazards

Separate biological, chemical and physical changes. Match each hazard or deterioration mechanism with a control that can actually address it.

HACCP reasoning

  1. Identify the significant hazard.
  2. Identify the critical control point.
  3. Set a measurable critical limit.
  4. Define monitoring, corrective action and records.

Labelling and transparency

Use evidence from ingredients, percentages, origin, health claims, additives, storage directions and third-party certification. Separate verified information from marketing cues.

Food formulation

A formulation includes component ratios, processes and structures. Ethical, time-poor, gourmet, solo and cultural consumers may require different solutions.

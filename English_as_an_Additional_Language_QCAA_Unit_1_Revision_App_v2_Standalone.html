Language, culture and context

Ask: Who made this text? For whom? For what purpose? In what social and cultural situation? Explain how those conditions shape the text’s choices and meaning.

Media representations

Distinguish fact, opinion, main ideas and supporting details. Look for selections, omissions, emphasis, visuals, editing, music and juxtaposition that position an audience.

Constructing purposeful texts

Use genre conventions, a suitable register, cohesive devices, accurate grammar and mode-appropriate features. Plan first; then draft, edit and proofread.

Response toolkit

For a media-text analysis, move beyond naming a feature. Use a clear chain: feature → example → effect → positioning → link to purpose/context.

  1. Identify the text’s purpose, audience, producer and relevant context.
  2. Develop a contention about its representation or perspective.
  3. Use precise evidence: wording, image, sound, layout or structural choice.
  4. Explain the effect, not just the technique.
  5. Edit for paragraph cohesion, vocabulary, grammar, spelling and punctuation.

Quick reference

Representation
A textual construction that gives shape to an idea, identity, time or place.
Positioning
How a text invites audiences to interpret or respond in particular ways.
Register
The degree of formality and language appropriate to purpose, audience and situation.
Cohesion
Connections that make ideas flow, including reference words, repetition, conjunctions and lexical chains.
Useful analytical verbs
Constructs, foregrounds, marginalises, contrasts, invites, challenges, reinforces, suggests.
